## The model

We do three things and we do them together, because none of them works alone.

**We publish and place Kreyòl books.** A child learns to read in the language spoken at home. Our six easy readers and our alphabet chart were written, illustrated and edited by Haitians, for Haitian children, about Haitian life.

**We build real libraries.** Shelves, light, power, internet, computers and a paid library worker. A safe, welcoming room where a child can sit and read after school, and where an older student can do homework in the evening.

**We train people.** Library workers complete an eleven module curriculum developed in Haiti. Teachers learn to read aloud, run a book corner and keep children curious. Training continues on Zoom and WhatsApp when travel is not possible.

## Where we are

- Duplan Methodist Community Library, Ouest. Full services since 2022.
- Cange Community Library, Centre. Full services.
- La Chapelle Community Library, Artibonite. Partial services.
- Ravine Sèche Library Center, Artibonite. Partial services.
- Summits Education Library System, about forty primary schools in the Centre. Partial services.

As a young nonprofit we cannot meet every request, and we keep a waiting list for service. That list is exactly why your support matters.

## Start a library in your village

A children's library begins with a village that wants one and will invest in it. If you want to work with PILH you will complete an application, join a Zoom or WhatsApp interview, and be selected by our review committee.

The village commits to four things:

1. Pay the library worker. We are glad to help you interview an enthusiastic candidate.
2. Maintain strong wi-fi for ongoing training sessions and village meetings.
3. Elect an active library committee of teachers and educated village leaders.
4. Develop realistic, workable ideas for sustainability.

A PILH project focuses on children from birth to eight years old. Once functional, it becomes the foundation for a larger community library serving older students and adults.

## A note on words

In Haiti, a library worker is the person in charge of a library, usually without formal certification, because the country has no accrediting program. A librarian holds a degree in library and information science. Haiti has only a few handfuls of certified librarians for eleven and a half million people. Changing that is part of our long game.
