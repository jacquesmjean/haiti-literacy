## Why Duplan

Over twelve years of service in Haiti we mentored and strengthened rural libraries in the Central Plateau and the Artibonite. The Duplan Community Library, in the Ouest, is different. It sits centrally in the country, semi rural and relatively secure, with great potential for service. We have developed it as a children's library serving ages birth to eight.

Methodist Pastor Edzaire Paul, the village leader and administrator, established the library more than twenty years ago and champions its modernization. A Memorandum of Agreement between PILH and the Duplan Village Governance Committee frames the project.

## The people

**Beverly Stephen** is the Lead Library Worker. Before receiving formal training she taught herself library skills from the internet. A high school graduate who studied administrative management at a Port-au-Prince university, she loves her job and her career goal is to become a librarian.

**Judeline Marechal** joined as assistant librarian in spring 2023. Her stated goal is to improve young children's education.

A library committee serves the project, chaired by Dr. Beatrice Gaunthier, a clinical internist, alongside the school director, community members and Pastor Paul.

## The village

The Duplan Methodist School serves roughly nine hundred students from pre kindergarten through twelfth grade, with the secondary level unique in the vicinity. The church and community are two hundred five years old. About five hundred families attend services, more than one hundred children sing in the children's choir, and neighboring villages come in for church and youth activities. All of them stand to benefit as the library grows.

Books circulate for one week to the teachers and students of the village school. A student's library card is their school identification card.

## The work

The training is led by Erick Toussaint, a well known former director of FOKAL's community library programs in Haiti. His eleven module curriculum covers the fundamentals of library administration and management. As trainees complete each module, progress is tested and reviewed with Erick over Zoom and WhatsApp.

The first task was weeding years of outdated books from the shelves. Four barely functioning laptops were replaced by six new Chromebooks. PILH purchased a large Kreyòl book collection for Duplan's students and teachers, in step with the Ministry of Education's decree that Kreyòl is the language of instruction.

Next, Beverly and Judeline will work with teachers to introduce new books, programs, activities and games that build the joy of reading. The goals for children and teachers are simple: progress toward school readiness, engage children in the joy of reading, and support the school curriculum. Parents will be welcomed in as the program matures.

## Accountability

The library committee funds the library's security and upkeep, the assistant's salary, strong wi-fi, furniture and shelving, electricity and water, and IT support. PILH provides funds as needs are identified and mutually agreed, plus an external evaluator who assesses progress each year. Funding depends on trackable results. That is how we treat every donated dollar.
