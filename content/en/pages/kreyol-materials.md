## Why we publish

In our field research we visited many rural schools and found a disturbing lack of teaching resources, especially books and alphabet charts. So we found partners and made them ourselves. Kreyòl speaking instructors delivered accompanying workshops to overflow crowds of teachers in village libraries. Independent evaluations came back very high, with teachers and trainers both requesting more.

## The six easy readers

**Jesula Ap Apran Li** (Jesula Learns to Read). Every year a woman named Carline visits Jesula's village with new books. Jesula learns to read, shares the gift with her family and community, and grows into a successful young woman. By Dr. Peter Manuel, illustrated by Jerry Rosenbert Moise, adapted by Milady Auguste.

**Malachi Bay Tout Vilaj La Manje** (Malachi Feeds the Village). Malachi finds seeds by the river and plants them. They become the main ingredient of soup joumou, and the village gathers to share the soup and stories of Haitian independence. By Cherilyn Flemming, illustrated by Enine Fleurantin, adapted by Milady Auguste.

**Serafina.** Serafina the donkey would rather play with children than work, until her friend Joseph finds the job that is perfect for her. By Kathryn Carter, illustrated by Jemson Guerrier, adapted by Milady Auguste.

**Istwa Konpé Kra Ak Konpé Po** (The Adventures of Mr. Kra and Mr. Po). Two frogs go on an adventure, get trapped in cream, and learn to never give up. By Dr. Peter Manuel, illustrated by Dishka Joseph, adapted by Milady Auguste.

**Bèl Zetwal Nan Syèl Ansdeno** (A Starry Night in Anse d'Hainault). Stella, the smallest star over Anse d'Hainault, learns that shining together beats shining alone. By Madeline Jenkins, illustrated by Anev, adapted by Milady Auguste.

**Mitan** (Half Time). While his friends play soccer, Frederic spots mangoes across the river. The perfect half time snack. By Ken Carter, illustrated by Garry Bathol, adapted by Milady Auguste.

The books grew from a conversation at the Haitian Kreyòl Academy and a Rotary International collaboration with the Rotary Club of Encounter Bay in Australia. Haitian linguists edited every page. Despite political unrest and Covid, training reached 25 schools, 95 teachers and 3,800 students over eight days of workshops.

## Alfabè Kreyòl Mwen, the alphabet chart

The alphabet is the first building block of reading, and until recently Haitian teachers and children had no chart with the correct phonemic sounds. Haitian Kreyòl blends Taino, French, African languages, English, Portuguese and Spanish, so it holds more letter sounds than many alphabets.

This chart took thirteen years of classroom testing and linguistic input. Drawn by Dan Linginfelter, field tested by Claude Saint-Natus and teachers across Haiti, shaped from its conception by Martin Blampied, and verified by Professor Michel DeGraff, director of the MIT Haiti Initiative and Fellow of the American Linguistic Society. It is copyrighted with the United States Library of Congress, registration VAu-1-427-475.

The charts come in wall and desk sizes, printed on vinyl, waterproof with UV inks, ideal for the tropics. Illustrations reflect imagery familiar to children across every region of Haiti.
