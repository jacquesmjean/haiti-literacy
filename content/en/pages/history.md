## The Rotary years

For eleven years we worked as an all volunteer Rotary Charities committee focused on village library development in Haiti's Central Plateau and the Artibonite. Until 2019 we regularly sent volunteers, donated more than 5,000 French and Kreyòl books, delivered early literacy training for teachers of kindergarten through early primary, and shared library management know how with library workers.

Between 2016 and 2021 a PILH volunteer graphic artist developed our Kreyòl alphabet chart. With authors from an Australian Rotary Club and a Haitian team of a coordinator, illustrators and translators, we co published six Kreyòl easy reader books. We supplied and trained 98 schools with charts and books. Fiscal support in those years came from Rotary Clubs, the nonprofit Haitian Education and Production Initiatives, three religious denominations and individual donors.

## Becoming a nonprofit

In 2021 we wrote a five year plan to become a small nonprofit dedicated to early literacy projects in rural Haiti. In September 2021 we received designation as a federal 501(c)(3), EIN 87-0904773, and began our work. Independence immediately improved our ability to attract talented people, pay a Haitian consultant team member, and raise funds for the growing number of project requests.

## Kreyòl becomes the language of the classroom

A milestone with national impact opened 2022. Haiti's Minister of Education, Nesmy Manigat, announced that beginning in September 2022, Kreyòl, the Haitian mother tongue, would be the language of instruction for kindergarten and primary education. French would be introduced later. Since all of our materials and training are in Kreyòl, the premise we had worked from for a decade became official national policy.

## The Duplan project begins

In February 2022, at the request of the Methodist Community Library in Duplan, we began a project to mentor its library workers and modernize the library with quality early literacy Kreyòl books, materials and technology. With only a few handfuls of certified librarians in the whole country, we plan with our collaborators to one day transform this centrally located building into a training facility that educates and advocates for more library workers across Haiti.
