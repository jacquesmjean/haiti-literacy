## The context

In 1697 Spain ceded the western third of Hispaniola to France, and that colony later became Haiti. Built on forestry and sugar, it was once among the wealthiest colonies in the Caribbean. Today Haiti is the poorest country in the Western Hemisphere and its least literate.

**Population.** About 11.3 million people. Kreyòl and French are the official languages. Kreyòl is spoken by 95 percent of the population, French by at most 10 percent.

**Economy.** About 80 percent live in poverty. The minimum wage is under 2.50 dollars a day, and two thirds of the labor force lack formal jobs. Two thirds of Haitians depend on small scale subsistence farming on land vulnerable to natural disasters and deforestation. Real GDP per capita was about 2,800 dollars in 2020. Source: CIA World Factbook.

Political unrest, violence, Covid and cholera kept many schools closed or late to open in recent years, especially in Port-au-Prince. In the Grand Sud, the August 2021 earthquake followed within two days by a tropical storm destroyed schools, churches and homes.

## What we take from this

Two numbers drive our strategy. Ninety five percent of Haitians speak Kreyòl, and pre primary enrollment sits near five percent. The earliest learners are the least served, in the language almost nobody prints books in. That is exactly where a children's library changes lives, and exactly where PILH works.
